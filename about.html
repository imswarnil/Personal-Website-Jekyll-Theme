The short version

I started out writing Apex on a Salesforce org that nobody wanted to touch, and found I liked the untouchable ones best. That turned into a decade around CRM Analytics, CPQ and the unglamorous plumbing between a quote and a paid invoice — dashboards that sales leaders actually open, SAQL that survives a fiscal-year change, quote flows that shave real hours off a deal.

Somewhere in the middle I picked up a camera. Editing taught me more about engineering than any book did: cut what doesn't serve the story, get the pacing right, and make the first three seconds count. Every dashboard, page and pull request I ship now runs through that same filter.

Today I split my time between shipping analytics at work, making travel and brand films under Imswarnil Studio, and writing about the craft of both here — guides, snippets, prompts and the occasional long essay.

What I'm good at

  • Analytics engineering — CRM Analytics, SAQL, data flows and the dashboards on top of them.
  • Quote-to-cash — Conga CPQ, quote automation, approval flows, pricing logic that finance can audit.
  • Web craft — Jekyll and Next.js sites, hand-written CSS systems (this one included), fast by default.
  • Film — writing, shooting, grading and cutting short-form films with a documentary sensibility.

01

Find the real question

Most "build me a dashboard" requests are really "help me make one decision." I start with the decision, the person making it, and what they'd do differently with a better answer.

02

Cut to the story

Editing discipline, applied to products: one clear message per screen, nothing on the page that isn't earning its place, and a first impression that lands in seconds.

03

Ship, measure, write it down

Small releases, real numbers, and a written record — so the next person (often future me) moves faster. Most of what's on this site started as that record.
